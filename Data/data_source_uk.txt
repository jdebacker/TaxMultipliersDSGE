UK DATA PIPELINE — companion notes
====================================

This UK pipeline mirrors the US pipeline (1a-1d_*.py) where the data exists
and is publicly accessible. Coverage and data sources differ from the US
in three substantive ways, summarized below.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pipeline files
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
    1b_build_data_uk.py       -> output/US_Data_Full_UK.xlsx
    1c_diagnose_data_uk.py    -> sanity checks (use US version with SRC swapped)
    1d_clean_for_matlab_uk.py -> output/US_Data_Matlab_UK.xlsx and .mat

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What's NOT in the UK build (and why)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. tau_l, tau_k, tau_c (effective tax rates)
   Achievable but requires its own module. Build by applying Mendoza-Razin-
   Tesar (1994) formulas to UK aggregates. Inputs needed:
     - OECD Revenue Statistics: detailed UK tax revenue by category
       (income tax, NICs, corporate tax, VAT/purchase tax, council tax)
     - ONS UK Sector Accounts: compensation of employees, mixed income,
       operating surplus, household final consumption
   Source URL pattern:
     https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/data/OECD.CTP.TPS,DSD_REV@DF_REV/
       GBR.....A?startPeriod=1955&format=csvfilewithlabels
   This is a separate file (e.g. 1b_build_data_uk_taxes.py) -- not yet built.
   Note that the UK introduced VAT in 1973; pre-1973 consumption tax was
   "Purchase Tax" with a different base, so a structural break around 1973
   is expected in tau_c.

2. tau_d, tau_i (dividend / interest income tax rates)
   No public UK equivalent of NBER TAXSIM. Practical options:
     (a) Proxy as tau_p (= tau_l from MRT formulas). Defensible, exact pre-1973
         since interest and dividends were taxed at ordinary income rates.
         Post-1973 (and especially post-2016 dividend allowance) this becomes
         an upper bound rather than exact.
     (b) Manually construct from HMRC statutory rate tables.
   Recommended: stick with (a) for parity with the US tau_p proxy approach.

3. tau_itc, e_tau (investment incentives)
   The UK does not have an analogous policy lever to the US ITC + bonus
   depreciation. UK uses "Capital Allowances" (Annual Investment Allowance,
   First Year Allowances, Full Expensing 2023+). These are conceptually
   different: AIA is a cap, not a rate. Constructing a UK e_tau-equivalent
   would require:
     - Statutory AIA caps and First Year Allowance percentages over time
     - HMRC-published average claim shares by asset type
   This is genuinely not directly comparable; the cleanest path is to
   omit these observables from the UK estimation and document the
   asymmetry. The fundamental tax mechanisms (tau_c, tau_l, tau_k, plus
   government spending and transfers) are the comparable subset.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Practical recommendation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a US-vs-UK comparison estimating the same DSGE on both:
    Run UK estimation with 8 observables: chat, ihat, ghat, bhat,
    pi_obs, r_obs, lhat, trhat.
    Tax shocks (eps_w, eps_k, eps_c, eps_d, eps_i, eps_ic, eps_etau) are
    structural in the model but not directly observed -- identified through
    priors and cross-equation restrictions on the 8 macro observables.

This matches the conservative spec used in your tax_dsge_est.mod (dropping
tau_d_hat and tau_i_hat from varobs) and extends it by also dropping
tau_l_hat, tau_k_hat, tau_c_hat, tau_itc_hat, e_tau_hat for the UK.
8 observables, still well above the threshold for stable Bayesian estimation
on 13 structural shocks.

If you later build the OECD-based UK tax module, you can add tau_l_hat,
tau_k_hat, tau_c_hat to UK varobs (giving 11 observables, matching the
US "extension" specification).
